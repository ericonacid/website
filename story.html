A note from Eric

Hello.
I'm Eric.
I'm a worm.

I know that much.

There are other things I know, and quite a few I'm not entirely sure about. I've been trying to put everything in order, but memories are funny things. Sometimes you remember the smell of something but not where you were. Sometimes you recognise a place you've never been. Sometimes you remember something quite clearly, only to discover that it couldn't possibly have happened.

I've decided not to worry about that too much.

I'll tell you what I know. I'll tell you what I think I know. And where there's a gap, I'll leave a gap.

There are some things that are probably best left alone.

We don't talk about those.

1. Before

I was an earthworm.

An ordinary one, as far as I know.

I liked soil. Damp soil, preferably. Good compost. Somewhere with plenty of oxygen and things breaking down nicely.

It's a pleasant way to spend your time.

I don't remember much else about being an ordinary worm. I couldn't tell you where I lived, who I knew, or whether I had any particular ambitions.

I expect I was quite content.

It's difficult to be certain about these things when you can't remember very much.

I do know that I didn't always look like this.

Or at least, I don't think I did.

2. Something Happened

There are things I remember about the night.

Light, perhaps.

A feeling of movement. Not the usual sort of movement. Not moving through soil or being moved by rain. Something altogether different.

I can't put a proper description to it.

There are fragments that don't seem to belong together.

I sometimes get the impression that there was something else there. Something happening around me. Something much bigger than me.

I don't know what it was.

I've heard words like Botanists and hyperspace mentioned since. They seem to have something to do with it.

I'm not sure I understand either.

Apparently, there was some sort of craft. Apparently, something happened to the space around it. Apparently, a number of things got caught up in the whole business.

I may have been one of them.

I say apparently because I wasn't taking notes at the time.

3. The Thing

There is something that happened.

I know that because everything afterwards was different.

I don't remember the actual event properly. Neither do the others, as far as I can tell.

There are suggestions that biological matter was somehow scrambled. That things were taken apart and put together again, although that sounds rather unpleasant when you put it like that.

I don't know what happened to me.

I don't know why I became the way I am.

I don't know why I have green spots, or why I feel so strongly that I'm called Eric.

I certainly don't remember agreeing to any of it.

There is a phrase we use when this subject comes up.

The Thing We Don't Talk About.

It seems to be understood.

I'm not sure whether we don't talk about it because we can't remember, or because remembering is something we'd rather avoid.

Possibly both.

Anyway, I don't want to dwell on it.

4. The Field

I woke up in a field.

There was something strange about the ground.

I remember the light first. Morning light. Then the smell of the plants and the earth. There was a shape around me, although I didn't immediately understand what it was.

A crop circle, apparently.

There were others.

I didn't know who they were. I didn't know where I was. I didn't know how I'd got there.

I did know that something wasn't right.

I looked at myself.

I was still a worm, but not quite as I remembered being one.

Orange. Green spots.

And a hat.

A bobble hat.

I don't know where it came from. I don't remember putting it on. I don't remember owning it before.

But it was there.

It felt like mine.

I decided to keep it.

There were other things to think about, but that seemed like a reasonable place to start.

Someone asked what had happened.

Nobody had an answer.

I think we all preferred not to discuss it.

5. The Others

There were several of us.

Another worm. A rather unusual little dinosaur. A tapeworm. A shark.

And a creature who seemed to be having some difficulty getting used to his new limbs.

We didn't know what had happened to each other.

We didn't know what had happened to ourselves.

But we seemed to know that we belonged together.

That's a strange feeling.

I can't explain it.

We decided to move uphill, towards the sunlight.

It seemed sensible.

We questioned practically everything, except each other.

I suppose that was the beginning of the gang.

6. Names

I already felt like an Eric.

I can't explain that either.

The other worm became Edwin.

The little dinosaur called herself Manni. She said it was short for Mandrew, although she didn't seem entirely sure why.

The tapeworm announced that he was Tappy.

The shark was given the name Rufus.

That was by the lugworm.

The lugworm then fell over while trying to coordinate his new limbs. The noise he made sounded a bit like Nigel.

So Nigel he became.

I thought it was a good name.

We were beginning to get somewhere.

Not necessarily somewhere we understood, but somewhere.

7. Over the Hill

We carried on uphill.

I remember the feeling of the ground changing beneath us. The light getting stronger. The others moving around me.

Eventually, we reached the top.

The crop circle was no longer visible behind us.

There was something ahead.

Something very strange.

A music festival.

I'd never seen anything like it.

There were people everywhere. Noise, colour, movement. Music coming from somewhere in the distance.

I found it rather interesting.

I still do, actually.

Music seemed to make sense in a way that quite a few other things didn't.

I don't know why.

We went towards it.

8. The Festival

I remember music.

I remember people.

I remember the feeling that there was an awful lot going on, and that most of it was quite enjoyable.

We had an extraordinary time.

I can't tell you everything that happened. Some of it is a little hazy.

There were moments when I felt as though I'd been somewhere before, even though I knew I hadn't.

There were sounds that seemed familiar.

And then there was the night.

It became rather heavy.

I don't know exactly when things started to get confused.

I don't know exactly what happened.

I remember fragments, but they don't join together particularly well.

I've tried putting them in order.

It hasn't helped.

9. After That

There's a gap.

I know there is because I can remember the festival, and I can remember waking up somewhere else.

But I can't properly account for everything in between.

I don't think the others can either.

We don't talk about it.

I sometimes wonder whether something happened that we all remember in different ways.

Or whether we all remember the same thing but have decided not to mention it.

I suppose it doesn't matter.

We were together.

That seemed to be the important part.

10. The Scrapyard

The next thing I remember properly is waking up in a scrapyard.

Manchester.

There were cars, bits of metal, old things and things that might have been useful if you knew what they were for.

There was a hut.

It had been made from all sorts of things. Toasters, kettles, car parts. An old snooker table had somehow become the roof.

There were chairs outside and a firepit.

The fire was always lit.

There was also an old television.

It worked.

I thought that was quite impressive.

Nigel seemed to know his way around the place.

I don't remember how we got there. I don't remember how it became his home. I don't remember why it felt familiar.

But it did.

There was something reassuring about it.

I had a feeling I'd be spending some time there.

11. The Beginning

So that's where I woke up.

A scrapyard in Manchester.

With Nigel, Edwin, Manni, Tappy, Rufus and the others.

I had a hat.

I had a few memories.

I had quite a few questions.

And I had no idea what had happened to me.

Since then, I've noticed things.

Places that seem familiar. Sounds that bring something back. Old drawings that look as though somebody knew us before we existed.

Some of them are signed DTR.

I don't know who DTR is.

Not yet.

But I think there may be more to this than I've been able to remember.

I'm going to keep looking.

Not too hard, mind you.

There's no point getting yourself worked up over something you can't quite remember.

Besides, I think there's something going on at the scrapyard.

And I may have left something there.

Possibly a doughnut.

Possibly my hat.

Actually, no.

I've got my hat.

Good.

That would have been a disaster.

ERIC

That's as much as I can remember.

For now.

That's as much as I can remember.
For now.
